Arbitrage

Demand reallocation from peak departures to alternatives where your network has room — recovering seat value before the door closes.

Request a Demo How It Works

Network view · rebalancing

The problem

Peak demand outruns boarding at your fare mix—while parallel legs still have room

Travelers hold bookings on departures that are now too tight for the fare mix you sold, while—on the same market day—parallel legs still have seats you need filled. Demand stays concentrated on the flight they purchased; it does not redistribute itself to match where the network has room.

Left unaddressed, teams reach for levers that trade yield for throughput or push risk to the gate: fare dilution, tightening overbooking, or last-moment improvisation. None of that captures the value represented by underloaded alternatives sitting next to an overloaded same-day departure.

How Vector surfaces it

Flagged departures, ranked by value

Arbitrage detects load, eligibility, and rule fit, then ranks voluntary moves before anyone acts—the same detect → decide → execute spine as the rest of Vector. Teams see who might move, to which alternative, and why the candidate survives policy checks.

Detect

Surface tight legs and alternatives with headroom before the window closes.

Decide

Only candidates that pass your rules advance; rationale stays visible for reviewers.

Execute

Writes and outreach stay on the governed path with a full audit record.

It runs on the same execution layer as your other agents—no parallel “special project” lists per corridor.

Governed execution

PSS and channels you already run

Governed execution: accepted changes flow through your passenger channels and booking stack under the same controls as the rest of Vector—confirmation where you require it, immutable logging, and clear boundaries for compensation or re-accommodation.

Reviewers can pause a lane, override a flight, or walk the audit trail when regulators or partners ask questions.

Who cares

Commercial and network teams, aligned

Commercial

Operations

See shifts hit the PSS early enough to staff and communicate—less improvisation at the hub.
