Vector · Security & Trust

Rule-constrained from signal to action.

Vector's governance model is architectural — not a policy layer retrofitted on top of a model. Every recommendation, every PSS write, and every passenger communication runs inside boundaries your airline defines and your compliance teams can audit.

01

Your rules execute — not Vector's judgment

Vector does not make autonomous decisions. Your airline configures the conditions under which each agent is allowed to act: offer windows, compensation thresholds, eligibility criteria, blackout periods, load boundaries, and channel sequences. Before any action executes, Vector checks every condition. If a rule is not satisfied, the action does not run — no exceptions, no overrides by the model.

This is not a black-box model with a guardrail bolted on. The rules are the execution layer. What Vector adds is the speed to evaluate them continuously, across your full network, in the time between signals and checkpoints.

02

Every decision is traceable

Every signal Vector detects, every score it computes, and every PSS write it executes is logged with a complete record that commercial, operations, and compliance stakeholders can reconstruct in seconds — not after a manual export.

  • Signal log What data Vector read, from which source, at what timestamp — so reviewers can verify the facts the decision was based on.
  • Decision rationale Which policy conditions were evaluated, which passed, which were the binding constraint — readable by humans, not just engineers.
  • PSS write record What was written to the booking record, the offer terms, the channel used, and the agent version that triggered it.
  • Outcome confirmation Whether the passenger accepted, what was confirmed, and the resulting revenue or operational effect — closed-loop evidence.

03

Your data stays yours

Vector processes the data required to execute reallocation: load and inventory signals, booking context, passenger eligibility, and operational flags. Airline data is never used to train shared models or shared with other carriers. Data residency, retention windows, and deletion schedules align with airline procurement requirements and are specified in the data processing agreement.

Access controls

Role-segmented access with airline-defined scopes. Volantio staff access is audited and limited to what is necessary for support and implementation.

No cross-carrier training

Your booking data, passenger profiles, and operational signals are isolated to your instance. They are not used to improve models for other airlines.

04

Connects to your existing stack — no migration required

Vector integrates with your PSS through certified, supported interface paths. It reads the signals it needs and writes back only the actions that passed policy evaluation. There is no requirement to replace, modify, or retrain staff on core reservation infrastructure.

Certified integrations exist for the platforms where most airline demand reallocation executes today. If your stack is not listed, the implementation team scopes connectivity before contracts are signed — not after.

05

Scoring you can explain to any stakeholder

Vector's ranking and scoring is a rule-plus-model hybrid — not a neural black box. Each recommendation carries a human-readable rationale: why this passenger, why this flight, which policy conditions were met, and what the expected revenue and operational effect is.

Revenue management, operations, and compliance reviewers see the same reasoning before any action executes. If the rationale does not satisfy a reviewer, they can hold, override, or adjust the rule that produced it — without needing to retrain a model.

"Why did Vector offer this passenger this flight?" — the answer is always in the audit log, expressed in the policy terms your team uses, not machine-learning weights.

06
